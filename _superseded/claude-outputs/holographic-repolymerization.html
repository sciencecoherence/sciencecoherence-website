3.1 Active TET1–3 demethylation & TDG base excision

Enzymatic erasure

Active reversal of age-associated hypermethylation drift is driven by Ten-Eleven Translocation (TET1, TET2, TET3) dioxygenases. Utilizing intracellular $\alpha$-ketoglutarate ($\alpha$-KG), $O_2$, and $Fe^{2+}$ cofactors, TET enzymes iteratively oxidize 5-methylcytosine ($5\text{mC}$):

$$5\text{mC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{hmC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{fC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{caC}$$

Thymine DNA Glycosylase (TDG) cleaves the $N$-glycosidic bond of $5\text{fC}$ and $5\text{caC}$, generating an apurinic/apyrimidinic (AP) abasic site. APE1 / Ref-1 then hydrolyzes the phosphodiester backbone $5'$ to the lesion, creating a single-strand break with a $3'\text{-OH}$ primer terminus and a blocking $5'\text{-dRP}$ flap.

In plain terms — the rust eraser

Think of DNA as a master cookbook. As cells get older, sticky chemical rust spots (methyl marks) gum up the recipe pages so the cell can’t read them. TET enzymes act like a rust converter, turning the hard rust into brittle chalk. Then TDG acts like a hole punch, popping the rusted letter clean out of the page so it is ready for a brand-new letter.

3.2 Physical gap resynthesis via Polymerase-$\beta$

BER gap-filling

DNA Polymerase $\beta$ ($\text{Pol }\beta$) executes Base Excision Repair (BER) gap filling via a bifunctional, dual-domain catalytic architecture:

  • $8\text{ kDa}$ amino lyase. Excises the blocking $5'\text{-dRP}$ residue via $\beta$-elimination to expose a clean $5'\text{-phosphate}$.
  • $31\text{ kDa}$ polymerase domain. Coordinates dual $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions to insert an unmodified deoxycytidine monophosphate (dCTP) opposite the template guanine:
$$\text{Gap}_{(3'\text{-OH} / 5'\text{-P})} + \text{dCTP} \xrightarrow{\text{Pol }\beta, \text{Mg}^{2+}} \text{DNA}_{\text{inserted}} + \text{PP}_i$$

The DNA Ligase III / XRCC1 complex seals the phosphodiester backbone, permanently restoring an unmethylated, youthful cytosine base.

In plain terms — the precision typewriter

After the rusted letter is popped out, the cell needs to replace it. Pol β is a tiny two-handed typewriter: its left hand snips away the torn paper edge, and its right hand stamps in a fresh, unblemished letter ‘C’ (dCTP). Molecular glue (DNA ligase) then seals the paper smooth.

3.3 Polymerase thiol redox preservation (DHLA axis)

Thiol redox

The catalytic pockets of $\text{Pol }\beta$, mitochondrial $\text{Pol }\gamma$, and endonuclease APE1 / Ref-1 rely on nucleophilic cysteine thiols (Cys267 on $\text{Pol }\beta$; Cys65 on Ref-1). Oxidative stress converts these thiols into sulfenic acids ($-\text{SOH}$) or disulfides, stalling repair processivity and triggering double-strand breaks.

$$\text{Enzyme}\text{-(S-S)} + \text{DHLA-(SH)}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Enzyme-(SH)}_2 + \text{R-ALA-(S-S)}$$

Dihydrolipoic acid (DHLA) — the intracellular reduced form of Na-R-ALA — possesses vicinal dithiol groups ($C6$ and $C8$) with a low redox potential ($E_0' = -0.32\text{ V}$), driving continuous thiol–disulfide exchange to keep repair polymerases active.

In plain terms — the machine gear oil

If cellular exhaust (free radicals) hits the typewriter’s moving parts, they rust and jam midway through typing, tearing the page in half. DHLA is high-grade anti-rust oil for the gears: it donates electrons continuously so the typing machinery never jams or breaks the DNA strand.

Interfacial water electrodynamics & quantum biophysics

QED & $H_3O_2^-$

1. Exclusion Zone ($H_3O_2^-$) lattice

Water in contact with hydrophilic macromolecular surfaces (collagen, actin, Tremella GXM) organizes into stacked hexagonal honeycomb sheets where oxygen and hydrogen arrange in a $3:2$ stoichiometry, building a net negative charge ($-100\text{ to } -200\text{ mV}$) while expelling solutes and hydronium ($H_3O^+$) protons.

2. Grotthuss proton conduction

Ordered EZ water chains act as biological proton wires ($H_2O + H^+ \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+$). Protons hop along hydrogen bonds with minimal thermal dissipation ($\Delta Q \approx 0$), delivering bioelectric charge directly to mitochondrial and nuclear enzymatic active sites.

3. 660 nm / 850 nm optical tuning

660 nm photolyzes inhibitory nitric oxide ($NO$) from Cytochrome c Oxidase (Complex IV), restoring electron flux. 850 nm resonates with water’s $O\text{--}H$ vibrational overtone, expanding the EZ lattice and reducing viscous drag around the $F_0F_1\text{-ATP}$ synthase rotor.

4.4 Fascial mechanics & electrokinetic streaming potentials ($V_{str}$)

Because collagen fibrils and proteoglycans possess a fixed negative charge density (FCD), mechanical shearing and stretching displace mobile hydronium counter-ions ($H_3O^+$) through the interstitial ground substance, generating an endogenous electric field governed by the Helmholtz–Smoluchowski equation:

$$V_{str} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \cdot \zeta \cdot \Delta P}{\mu \cdot \sigma}$$

Where $\zeta$ is the zeta potential, $\Delta P$ is the hydrostatic pressure gradient, $\mu$ is ground-substance viscosity, and $\sigma$ is electrical conductivity. This streaming voltage depolarizes fibroblast membranes, opening Piezo1, Piezo2, and TRPV4 stretch-activated $Ca^{2+}$ channels to stimulate de novo collagen and hyaluronic-acid synthesis.

5.1 MFSD2A transport & the Lands cycle
